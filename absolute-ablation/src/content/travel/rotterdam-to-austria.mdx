We're taking our family — three adults, a five-year-old, and a one-year-old — on a road trip from Rotterdam to Austria in May 2026. Eight days, one overnight transit through Würzburg, one night at Krimml village (Europe's highest waterfall), then four nights at Bad Ischl as a base for mountains and caves in the Salzkammergut. No stroller — baby carrier only. This post is the full plan — day-by-day itinerary, costs, packing lists, and everything I've learned while researching.

<SectionHeading icon="fa-route" color="blue">The Route</SectionHeading>

<div class="prose-content">

Our route heads south from Rotterdam through Würzburg (Franconia) to Krimml village for Europe's highest waterfall, then into Austria's Salzkammergut for four nights at Bad Ischl, then back the same way. Every driving leg stays under five hours. Bad Ischl is the hub — 20-25 minutes from every day trip destination.

<Callout type="insight" title="Weather-based day swapping" icon="fa-cloud-sun">
  <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Days 5–6 (Waterfalls & Lakes, Dachstein) can be reordered to chase the best weather. Day 3 (Krimml) is fixed — it's on the transit day. Clear days go to Dachstein Five Fingers and Altausseer See. Grey or rainy days go to the Mammut Cave and Waldbachstrub (waterfalls work in rain). Krimml waterfalls work in any weather — they're actually more spectacular in rain.</p>

<Callout type="warning" title="Supply strategy" icon="fa-shopping-cart">
  <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Day 2 (Sunday) and Day 3 (Whit Monday) both have shops closed. <strong>Order nappies, baby supplies, and essentials online before the trip and pack them.</strong> Stock up in Würzburg on Saturday (Day 1) before shops close (~8 PM). First Austrian shop run: Tuesday (Day 4) at Spar/Billa in Bad Ischl. Petrol station shops remain open for emergencies.</p>

Arrive Krimml village late afternoon. Check into your Ferienwohnung or Gasthaus (~€80-140/night, shoulder season). The <strong>Gerlos Alpenstraße (B165) has a separate toll of ~€10</strong> per vehicle — not covered by the vignette. The toll-free alternative via Kitzbühel/Mittersill adds ~20-30min.</p>

<StepList>
  <StepItem number={3} title="★ Krimml Waterfalls — Mon 25 May (Whit Monday) — FLAGSHIP DAY">
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-600 leading-relaxed"><strong>This is the #1 priority day of the trip.</strong> Krimml is Europe's highest waterfall — 380m in three thundering stages. Whit Monday means tourist attractions are open but tour buses don't arrive until ~10 AM. Walk to the falls at 08:00 from the village (5min). The <strong>first viewpoint (Kürsingerplatz)</strong> is only 15min from the entrance on a <strong>barrier-free, paved path — fully senior-friendly</strong>. This is the most dramatic single drop: 140m of water with mist spray reaching the platform. The senior and baby stay here. One adult + the 5-year-old continue to the <strong>2nd viewpoint (Riemann Kanzel, 30min)</strong> and optionally the <strong>3rd viewpoint (Regen Kanzel, 45min)</strong> — steeper with steps. Done by 10:00-10:30.</p>

<Callout type="insight" title="Why Bad Ischl over St. Wolfgang?" icon="fa-lightbulb">
  <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">St. Wolfgang is charming but Bad Ischl saves <strong>35min each way to Dachstein</strong> and is 20-25min from Waldbachstrub and Altausseer See. <Callout type="insight" title="Not winter gear — spring layers" icon="fa-tshirt">

<SectionHeading icon="fa-circle-question" color="purple">FAQ</SectionHeading>

<div class="space-y-4 my-6">
  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Why Bad Ischl over St. Wolfgang as a base?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Bad Ischl is 20-25 minutes from every day trip destination. St. Wolfgang saves time only on Schafberg day (walk vs 20min drive) but costs 35 min extra each way to Dachstein. Bad Ischl is also cheaper (€90-150 vs €120-200/night) and has real services — supermarkets, pharmacy, playgrounds. Krimml is handled on the transit in, not from the base.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Why Mammut Cave over the Ice Cave?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">The Mammut Cave is +3°C (manageable with a fleece). The Ice Cave is -2°C with 500-700 steep stairs and a 30m rope bridge — demanding for a senior and requires snowsuit-level warmth for the baby. Same quality experience, much more family-friendly. Only choose the Ice Cave if the senior is fit and you've packed for sub-zero temperatures.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Can I take a baby into the Dachstein caves?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Yes — there's no lower age limit. You'll need a baby carrier (no strollers). For the Mammut Cave (+3°C), dress the baby in a warm onesie + hat + blanket. The tour is 50 minutes. Watch for low ceilings when wearing the carrier.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Is Krimml senior-friendly with a baby?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">The first viewpoint (Kürsingerplatz) is 15min from the entrance on a barrier-free, paved path — fully accessible. This is also the most dramatic viewpoint (140m unbroken drop with mist spray). The senior and baby can stay here comfortably. The upper trail gets steeper with steps — one adult and the 5-year-old can continue while others wait.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Should I book Dachstein tickets in advance?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Yes — Dachstein is popular in late May with limited cave tour slots. Book combo tickets at <a href="https://www.dachstein-salzkammergut.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-blue-600 underline">dachstein-salzkammergut.com</a>. The Waterfalls & Lakes day (Waldbachstrub + Altausseer See) needs no bookings at all — both are free and open access.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">What if the weather is bad on Dachstein day?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">The Mammut Cave works in any weather — it's inside a mountain. Skip the Five Fingers viewpoint if visibility is zero (no point without the view). You can swap Dachstein and Waterfalls & Lakes days based on weather — Altausseer See is prettier in sunshine, Waldbachstrub waterfall works in rain, Dachstein caves don't need clear skies.</p>

You need one fleece + windproof jacket per person for mountain summits (5-12°C) and the Mammut Cave (+3°C). Light gloves and hat for cave day. No winter coats, thermals, or ski gear. The baby needs a warm onesie + hat for cave/summit days, not a snowsuit.</p>
