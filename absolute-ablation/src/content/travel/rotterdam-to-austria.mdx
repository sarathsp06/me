We're taking our family — three adults, a five-year-old, and a one-year-old — on a road trip from Rotterdam to Austria in May 2026. Eight days, one overnight transit through Würzburg, then five nights at Bad Ischl as a base for caves, mountains, and lakes in the Salzkammergut. No stroller — baby carrier only. This post is the full plan — day-by-day itinerary, costs, packing lists, and everything I've learned while researching.

    <p class="text-sm text-gray-600 leading-relaxed"><strong>No driving. No tickets. Three options: <strong>Katrin Cable Car</strong> (in Bad Ischl — gentle alpine meadows at the summit, 5-12°C, easy walks), <strong>Wolfgangsee</strong> (15min drive to Strobl — lakeside promenade, boat rides, swimmable ~17-19°C), or <strong>just Bad Ischl</strong> (Kaiservilla grounds, Kurpark playground, find your favourite dinner spots for the week).</p>

  <StepItem number={4} title="Schafberg Cog Railway — Tue 26 May">
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-600 leading-relaxed"><strong>20min drive</strong> to St. Wolfgang Talstation. A historic steam cogwheel railway climbs to the <strong>Schafberg summit (1,732m)</strong> — panoramic views over 7 lakes. ~35min up. <strong>Book the first departure</strong> (usually 09:00–09:15) and be at the Talstation by 08:30. Strollers are NOT allowed — baby carrier only (stored at the station). At the summit: fenced terrace with restaurant, 50m climb on uneven terrain to the very top. ~5-12°C and windy — warm layers essential.</p>

Not a consolation prize: genuine mountain hut, panoramic views, easy terrain. The senior can wait here while you take the 5-year-old to the summit on the next train.</p>

<Callout type="insight" title="Why Bad Ischl over St. Wolfgang?" icon="fa-lightbulb">
  <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">St. Wolfgang is charming but only saves time on Schafberg day (walk vs 20min drive). Bad Ischl saves <strong>30min each way to Dachstein</strong> (your flagship day) and <strong>25min each way to Hallstatt</strong>. Over the trip, that's ~2h less driving with the kids. It's also cheaper (€90-150 vs €120-200/night) and has real services — supermarkets, pharmacy, playgrounds — not tourist-only shops.</p>
</Callout>

<SectionHeading icon="fa-circle-question" color="purple">FAQ</SectionHeading>

<div class="space-y-4 my-6">
  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Why Bad Ischl over St. Wolfgang as a base?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Bad Ischl is 25-35 minutes from every day trip destination. St. Wolfgang saves time only on Schafberg day (walk vs 20min drive) but costs 30 min extra each way to Dachstein (your flagship day) and 25 min extra to Hallstatt. Bad Ischl is also cheaper (€90-150 vs €120-200/night) and has real services — supermarkets, pharmacy, playgrounds.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Why Mammut Cave over the Ice Cave?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">The Mammut Cave is +3°C (manageable with a fleece). The Ice Cave is -2°C with 500-700 steep stairs and a 30m rope bridge — demanding for a senior and requires snowsuit-level warmth for the baby. Same quality experience, much more family-friendly. Only choose the Ice Cave if the senior is fit and you've packed for sub-zero temperatures.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Can I take a baby into the Dachstein caves?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Yes — there's no lower age limit. You'll need a baby carrier (no strollers). For the Mammut Cave (+3°C), dress the baby in a warm onesie + hat + blanket. The tour is 50 minutes. Watch for low ceilings when wearing the carrier.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Is the Hallstatt salt mine open?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Salzwelten Hallstatt is under renovation until "summer 2026" — it may not be open by late May. Check <a href="https://www.salzwelten.at/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-blue-600 underline">salzwelten.at</a> before booking. If closed, a free shuttle to Altaussee salt mine is available. The village itself is still worth visiting — 2-3 hours for the lakefront and photos.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Should I book Schafberg and Dachstein tickets in advance?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Yes — both are popular in late May with limited daily capacity. Book Schafberg first-morning departure at <a href="https://www.schafbergbahn.at/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-blue-600 underline">schafbergbahn.at</a>. Book Dachstein combo tickets at <a href="https://www.dachstein-salzkammergut.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-blue-600 underline">dachstein-salzkammergut.com</a>.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">What if the weather is bad on Dachstein day?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">The Mammut Cave works in any weather — it's inside a mountain. Skip the Five Fingers viewpoint if visibility is zero (no point without the view). Dachstein day is the most weather-resilient day on the itinerary.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Will we need winter gear?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">No. Late May in the Salzkammergut is late spring (15-22°C in valleys). You need one fleece + windproof jacket per person for mountain summits (5-12°C) and the Mammut Cave (+3°C). Light gloves and hat for cave day. No winter coats, thermals, or ski gear. The baby needs a warm onesie + hat for cave/summit days, not a snowsuit.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Is Austria cheaper than Switzerland for a family trip?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Significantly. The motorway vignette is €13 vs CHF 40. Mountain attractions are roughly half the Swiss price. Eating out is €12–20 vs CHF 25–40. Accommodation at Bad Ischl is €90–150/night vs €200–400/night in Swiss lake towns. Self-catering saves further.</p>
