We're taking our family — three adults, a five-year-old, and a one-year-old — on a road trip from Rotterdam to Austria in May 2026. Eleven days, six stops, one loop through the Rhine Valley, medieval Germany, and the Austrian Salzkammergut. The plan: five nights at Wolfgangsee as a base for caves, mountains, and lakes. This post is the full plan — day-by-day itinerary, costs, packing lists, and everything I've learned while researching.

<SectionHeading icon="fa-route" color="blue">The Route</SectionHeading>

<div class="prose-content">

<StepList>
  <StepItem number={1} title="Rotterdam → Koblenz — Fri 22 May">
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-600 leading-relaxed"><strong>3.5–4h drive</strong> south via the A61. Koblenz sits at the confluence of the Rhine and Mosel — the <strong>Deutsches Eck</strong> headland is a UNESCO World Heritage site. Flat Rhine promenades, great for strollers. The <strong>cable car across the Rhine to Ehrenbreitstein Fortress</strong> is a panoramic start to the trip — flat pathways and viewpoints on top. Keep walking distances short from parking — this is a warm-up day.</p>
    <div class="mt-2 p-3 bg-amber-50 border border-amber-200 rounded-lg">
      <p class="text-xs font-semibold text-amber-700 mb-1"><i class="fas fa-gas-pump"></i> Recommended rest stop</p>
      <p class="text-xs text-amber-600 mb-0">Raststätte Mosel (A61, near Koblenz) — playground, baby-change facilities, about 2–2.5h into the drive.</p>
    </div>
  </StepItem>
</StepList>

<StepList>
  <StepItem number={2} title="Koblenz → Rothenburg ob der Tauber — Sat 23 May">
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-600 leading-relaxed"><strong>2.5–3h drive</strong> via A3/A7. Arrive by early afternoon. Rothenburg is one of Germany's best-preserved medieval towns — <strong>walk along the town walls</strong>, see the iconic <strong>Plönlein</strong> corner, and explore the cobblestone old town. The town empties by evening when day-trippers leave — the best time to enjoy it. Optional: the <strong>Night Watchman tour</strong> (20:00, English) — a costumed guide walks you through the old town by lantern light.</p>
    <div class="mt-2 p-3 bg-emerald-50 border border-emerald-200 rounded-lg">
      <p class="text-xs font-semibold text-emerald-700 mb-1"><i class="fas fa-shopping-cart"></i> Saturday — shops open!</p>
      <p class="text-xs text-emerald-600 mb-0"><strong>Stock up at DM/Rossmann today</strong> (en route or near Rothenburg). Day 3 is Sunday (closed) and Day 4 is Whit Monday (also closed). This is your last chance for a full supply run until Tuesday.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="mt-2 p-3 bg-blue-50 border border-blue-200 rounded-lg">
      <p class="text-xs font-semibold text-blue-700 mb-1"><i class="fas fa-gift"></i> Kids love it</p>
      <p class="text-xs text-blue-600 mb-0"><strong>Käthe Wohlfahrt Christmas Museum</strong> — year-round Christmas shop with ornaments, decorations, and a magical atmosphere. Free to browse.</p>
    </div>
  </StepItem>
  <StepItem number={3} title="Rothenburg → St. Wolfgang — Sun 24 May">
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-600 leading-relaxed"><strong>4–4.5h drive</strong> — the longest southbound leg, via A6/A9/A1. Plan two rest stops with playgrounds and baby-change. This is a travel day — arrive at St. Wolfgang, settle in, short lakefront walk only.</p>
    <div class="mt-2 p-3 bg-rose-50 border border-rose-200 rounded-lg">
      <p class="text-xs font-semibold text-rose-700 mb-1"><i class="fas fa-exclamation-triangle"></i> Sunday — shops closed</p>
      <p class="text-xs text-rose-600 mb-0">Shops are closed on Sundays in Germany and Austria. Ensure supplies were bought on Saturday (Day 2). Petrol station shops remain open for essentials.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="mt-2 p-3 bg-amber-50 border border-amber-200 rounded-lg">
      <p class="text-xs font-semibold text-amber-700 mb-1"><i class="fas fa-road"></i> Austrian motorway vignette</p>
      <p class="text-xs text-amber-600 mb-0">Required for Austrian motorways. Buy the <strong>10-day digital vignette</strong> online at <a href="https://www.asfinag.at/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-amber-700 underline">asfinag.at</a> before departure (~€9.90). Much cheaper than Switzerland's CHF 40.</p>
    </div>
  </StepItem>
</StepList>

<StepList>
  <StepItem number={4} title="Wolfgangsee — Lake / Settling Day — Mon 25 May (Whit Monday)">
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-600 leading-relaxed"><strong>No driving. No tickets. No time pressure.</strong> Recover from three days of driving. Today is <strong>Whit Monday</strong> — a public holiday, so shops are closed, but that doesn't matter for a lake day. Tourist attractions (boats, cable cars) typically operate on public holidays. Take the <strong>Wolfgangsee boat</strong> between St. Wolfgang, St. Gilgen, and Strobl — flat docks, stroller-friendly. Walk the <strong>St. Wolfgang lakeside promenade</strong>, see the White Horse Inn (Weisses Rössl), find a lakeside playground. Wolfgangsee is swimmable in late May (~17–19°C) if weather cooperates. This is a "low cognitive load" day — just lake, village, and rest.</p>
  </StepItem>
  <StepItem number={5} title="Schafberg Cog Railway — Tue 26 May">
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-600 leading-relaxed"><strong>The Austrian Mount Rigi.</strong> A historic steam cogwheel railway climbs from St. Wolfgang to the <strong>Schafberg summit (1,783m)</strong>. ~35min up. <strong>Book the first or second train</strong> (usually 9:00–9:30) for best visibility. Flat, fenced summit terrace with restaurant and benches — safe for kids. Panoramic views over 7 lakes including Wolfgangsee, Mondsee, and Attersee. ~10–12°C at the summit — warm layers essential, especially for the baby.</p>
    <div class="mt-2 p-3 bg-blue-50 border border-blue-200 rounded-lg">
      <p class="text-xs font-semibold text-blue-700 mb-1"><i class="fas fa-cloud-rain"></i> Weather swap</p>
      <p class="text-xs text-blue-600 mb-0">Schafberg needs clear skies. If cloudy, swap with Hallstatt or Salzburg.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="mt-2 p-3 bg-amber-50 border border-amber-200 rounded-lg">
      <p class="text-xs font-semibold text-amber-700 mb-1"><i class="fas fa-ticket-alt"></i> Cost</p>
      <p class="text-xs text-amber-600 mb-0">Adults ~€42 return, children 6–14 ~€25. Under 6 free. Book at <a href="https://www.schafbergbahn.at/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-amber-700 underline">schafbergbahn.at</a>.</p>
    </div>
  </StepItem>
</StepList>

<StepList>
  <StepItem number={6} title="Hallstatt Day Trip — Wed 27 May">
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-600 leading-relaxed"><strong>~45min each way.</strong> Austria's #1 tourist attraction — a UNESCO World Heritage village wedged between mountains and lake. <strong>Arrive before 10am</strong> to beat the crowds. Park at P1 (tunnel entrance) — the village centre is car-free. Walk through village lanes, lakeside photos, lake boat crossing. Then the <strong>salt mine (Salzwelten) + Skywalk</strong> — Europe's oldest salt mine with underground slides kids love, plus a viewing platform 350m above the lake.</p>
    <div class="mt-2 p-3 bg-rose-50 border border-rose-200 rounded-lg">
      <p class="text-xs font-semibold text-rose-700 mb-1"><i class="fas fa-baby"></i> Split plan for the salt mine</p>
      <p class="text-xs text-rose-600 mb-0"><strong>Minimum age: 4 years.</strong> One adult + Ishani (5) do the mine. One adult stays at the panoramic terrace/Skywalk with baby Saanvi. Baby carrier needed for Skywalk area (some steps).</p>
    </div>
  </StepItem>
</StepList>

<StepList>
  <StepItem number={7} title="Dachstein Krippenstein — Thu 28 May — ★ FLAGSHIP DAY">
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-600 leading-relaxed"><strong>~60min each way.</strong> This is the #1 priority day of the trip. Drive to Obertraun for the Dachstein Krippenstein cable car.</p>
    <div class="mt-3 space-y-2">
      <div class="p-3 bg-blue-50 border border-blue-200 rounded-lg">
        <p class="text-xs font-semibold text-blue-700 mb-1"><i class="fas fa-icicles"></i> 1. Giant Ice Cave (Rieseneishöhle)</p>
        <p class="text-xs text-blue-600 mb-0">Enormous ice formations inside the mountain. Guided tour ~1h. <strong>0°C year-round</strong> — warm layers essential for everyone, especially the baby. <strong>Many stairs, no stroller — baby carrier required.</strong> Works in any weather.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="p-3 bg-emerald-50 border border-emerald-200 rounded-lg">
        <p class="text-xs font-semibold text-emerald-700 mb-1"><i class="fas fa-hand-point-right"></i> 2. Five Fingers Viewpoint</p>
        <p class="text-xs text-emerald-600 mb-0">Five metal platforms jutting out over a 400m drop. Glass floor on one. Flat walk from top station (~20min). Stroller-friendly path. <strong>Skip if heavy cloud — no point without the view.</strong> Keep older kid close at railings.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="p-3 bg-purple-50 border border-purple-200 rounded-lg">
        <p class="text-xs font-semibold text-purple-700 mb-1"><i class="fas fa-mountain"></i> 3. Mammut Cave (optional)</p>
        <p class="text-xs text-purple-600 mb-0">Second cave nearby with rock formations. Only if time and energy allow.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="mt-2 p-3 bg-amber-50 border border-amber-200 rounded-lg">
      <p class="text-xs font-semibold text-amber-700 mb-1"><i class="fas fa-ticket-alt"></i> Cost</p>
      <p class="text-xs text-amber-600 mb-0">Cable car + ice cave combo: adults ~€50, children ~€28. <strong>Book combo tickets in advance</strong> at <a href="https://www.dachstein-salzkammergut.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-amber-700 underline">dachstein-salzkammergut.com</a>.</p>
    </div>
  </StepItem>
  <StepItem number={8} title="Salzburg — Flex Day — Fri 29 May">
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-600 leading-relaxed"><strong>~45min each way. This day is flexible</strong> — can be swapped for a nature excursion if the family wants more nature over city. Default plan: <strong>Hohensalzburg Fortress</strong> (funicular up, flat ramparts, panoramic views), <strong>Mirabell Gardens</strong> (Sound of Music "Do-Re-Mi" steps — kids can run around), <strong>Getreidegasse</strong> (old town shopping street, Mozart's birthplace), and the <strong>Salzach River promenade</strong>. An easy half-day — leave by mid-afternoon to enjoy the evening back at St. Wolfgang.</p>
    <div class="mt-2 p-3 bg-amber-50 border border-amber-200 rounded-lg">
      <p class="text-xs font-semibold text-amber-700 mb-1"><i class="fas fa-parking"></i> Parking</p>
      <p class="text-xs text-amber-600 mb-0">P+R Salzburg Süd (~€4/day, shuttle to centre) or central Altstadt garage.</p>
    </div>
  </StepItem>
</StepList>

<SectionHeading icon="fa-road" color="gray">The Return</SectionHeading>

<StepList>
  <StepItem number={9} title="St. Wolfgang → Regensburg — Sat 30 May">
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-600 leading-relaxed"><strong>~3h drive</strong> north via A1/A8/A93. Late-morning departure. Regensburg is a UNESCO World Heritage city — less touristy than Rothenburg but equally beautiful. Walk the <strong>Stone Bridge (Steinerne Brücke)</strong> — an 850-year-old bridge over the Danube. Lunch at the <strong>Historic Sausage Kitchen (Historische Wurstkuchl)</strong> — Germany's oldest fast-food restaurant, grilling sausages since 1146. Then the medieval old town and a <strong>Danube promenade</strong> evening stroll. Flat and compact — good for strollers.</p>
  </StepItem>
  <StepItem number={10} title="Regensburg → Königswinter — Sun 31 May">
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-600 leading-relaxed"><strong>~4h drive</strong> via A3. The <strong>Drachenfels cog railway</strong> in Königswinter (Germany's oldest) is a fitting bookend to the trip — a short ride to a castle ruin with Rhine valley views. A "mini mountain + river" finale. Flat <strong>Rhine promenade</strong> for an evening walk.</p>
    <div class="mt-2 p-3 bg-amber-50 border border-amber-200 rounded-lg">
      <p class="text-xs font-semibold text-amber-700 mb-1"><i class="fas fa-gas-pump"></i> Recommended rest stop</p>
      <p class="text-xs text-amber-600 mb-0">Somewhere around the halfway mark (~2h in) on the A3 — playground and baby-change.</p>
    </div>
  </StepItem>
  <StepItem number={11} title="Königswinter → Rotterdam — Mon 1 Jun">
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-600 leading-relaxed"><strong>2.5–3h drive.</strong> Early departure, home before or just after lunch. Travel day only — minimal sightseeing.</p>
    <div class="mt-2 p-3 bg-amber-50 border border-amber-200 rounded-lg">
      <p class="text-xs font-semibold text-amber-700 mb-1"><i class="fas fa-gas-pump"></i> Recommended rest stop</p>
      <p class="text-xs text-amber-600 mb-0">Raststätte Köln-Eifeltor or Frechen (A1/A4 area, ~45min in) — playground, baby-change.</p>
    </div>
  </StepItem>
</StepList>

<SectionHeading icon="fa-circle-question" color="purple">FAQ</SectionHeading>

<div class="space-y-4 my-6">
  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Do I need an Austrian motorway vignette?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Yes. Any car using Austrian motorways needs a vignette. The 10-day digital vignette costs ~€9.90 — buy it online at <a href="https://www.asfinag.at/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-blue-600 underline">asfinag.at</a> before departure, or at a border petrol station. Much cheaper than the Swiss vignette (CHF 40).</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Can I take a baby into the Dachstein Ice Cave?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Yes — there's no lower age limit (confirmed by official FAQ). However, it's 0°C inside with many stairs and no stroller access. You'll need a baby carrier and warm layers (snowsuit for the baby). It's physically demanding. One option: one adult stays outside with the baby while others do the cave.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">What's the minimum age for the Hallstatt salt mine?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600"><strong>4 years old.</strong> Ishani (5) can enter, but Saanvi (1) cannot. Plan a split: one adult + Ishani do the mine tour, one adult stays at the panoramic terrace/Skywalk with the baby.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Should I book Schafberg and Dachstein tickets in advance?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Yes — both are popular in late May with limited daily capacity. Book Schafberg early morning departure at <a href="https://www.schafbergbahn.at/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-blue-600 underline">schafbergbahn.at</a>. Book Dachstein combo tickets at <a href="https://www.dachstein-salzkammergut.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-blue-600 underline">dachstein-salzkammergut.com</a>.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Why St. Wolfgang over Hallstatt as a base?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">St. Wolfgang is cheaper, quieter, and centrally located. Hallstatt is a day-trip destination — crowded, limited parking, expensive accommodation, and not ideal for a 5-night base. St. Wolfgang has the Schafberg cog railway starting in the village, regular boat connections, flat promenades, and is 45–60min from all major excursions.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">What if the weather is bad on Dachstein day?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">The ice cave works in any weather — it's inside a mountain. Skip the Five Fingers viewpoint if visibility is zero (no point without the view). The Mammut Cave is another indoor option. Dachstein day is the most weather-resilient day on the itinerary.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Can I take a stroller on the Schafberg cog railway?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Fold it before boarding — the carriages are tight. At the summit, the main terrace is flat but limited. A baby carrier is more practical for the summit. Bring both: foldable stroller for the village, carrier for the mountain.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Is Austria cheaper than Switzerland for a family trip?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Significantly. The motorway vignette is €10 vs CHF 40. Mountain attractions are roughly half the Swiss price. Eating out is €12–20 vs CHF 25–40. Accommodation target is €100–200/night vs €200–400/night. Self-catering at Wolfgangsee saves even more.</p>
    </div>
  </details>
