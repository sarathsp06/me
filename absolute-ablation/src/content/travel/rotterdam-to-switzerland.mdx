We're taking our family — three adults, a five-year-old, and a one-year-old — on a road trip from Rotterdam to Switzerland in May 2026. Nine days, five stops, one loop through the Rhine Valley, the Black Forest, and Lake Lucerne. I've spent weeks planning this route to keep every driving leg under four hours, avoid steep hikes, and build in enough rest days that nobody melts down (adults included). This post is the full plan — day-by-day itinerary, costs, packing lists, and everything I wish someone had written down before I started researching.

<SectionHeading icon="fa-route" color="blue">The Route</SectionHeading>

<div class="prose-content">

Our route heads south from Rotterdam through Germany, into Switzerland for five nights at Lake Lucerne, then back north via Königswinter on the Rhine. Every driving leg stays under four hours — manageable with young children and nap schedules.

Gentle summit paths, viewpoints, and a mountain playground at the top. ~12°C at the summit — bring layers. Flat summit terrace with restaurant. This is one of the days I'm most looking forward to — the views from Rigi across the lake are supposed to be extraordinary.</p>

  <StepItem number={6} title="Lucerne City by Boat — Thu 28 May">

In Lucerne: <strong>Chapel Bridge (Kapellbrücke)</strong> — flat, stroller-friendly. Old town wander (some cobblestones on side streets, main routes are fine). Lakefront promenade. Boat back in the afternoon.</p>

<StepList>
  <StepItem number={7} title="Mount Pilatus — Golden Round Trip — Fri 29 May">
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-600 leading-relaxed"><strong>No driving.</strong> The "golden round trip" — a circular route combining boat, cable car, and the world's steepest cogwheel railway. This is the marquee day of the trip. Lake steamer to Alpnachstad (~90 min), cogwheel railway to Pilatus Kulm summit (2,132m), panoramic Alpine views. Flat, fenced summit terrace with restaurant. Descend by gondola and aerial cableway to Kriens, then bus/taxi back to the Lucerne boat pier for the steamer home.</p>
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-600 leading-relaxed mt-2"><strong>Note:</strong> The cogwheel railway (Alpnachstad side) typically opens late April/early May — by 29 May it should be fully running. Confirm on the <a href="https://www.pilatus.ch/en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-blue-600 underline">Pilatus website</a>. Strollers fit on boats and gondolas; fold for the cogwheel carriages.</p>
    <div class="mt-2 p-3 bg-blue-50 border border-blue-200 rounded-lg">

North through Switzerland back into Germany via Basel, then up the Rhine corridor on the A5/A3. <strong>Königswinter</strong> is a small Rhine-side town opposite Bonn, nestled under Drachenfels hill. Flat river promenade, easy parking. The <strong>Drachenfels cogwheel railway</strong> (Germany's oldest) is a nice bookend to the trip — a short ride to a castle ruin with Rhine valley views before the final drive home.</p>

<SectionHeading icon="fa-mountain" color="emerald">Lake Lucerne: The Main Base</SectionHeading>

<div class="prose-content">

Five nights at Lake Lucerne is the heart of the trip. Pick a lakeside village as a base for everything — the Rigi day trip, the Pilatus golden round trip, the Lucerne city boat day, and a rest day. Both Weggis and Vitznau have flat waterfront paths — good for strollers and anyone who prefers level ground.

</div>

<Callout type="insight" title="Our pick" icon="fa-lightbulb">
  <p class="text-sm text-gray-600"><strong>Vitznau</strong> edges it for families with young kids. The cogwheel railway is a highlight, the village is quieter (better for naps), and the flat station-to-boat connection makes day trips seamless. Weggis is better if you want more dinner options. Both villages are a 10-minute boat ride apart.</p>
</Callout>

<SectionHeading icon="fa-circle-question" color="purple">FAQ</SectionHeading>

<div class="space-y-4 my-6">
  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Do I need a Swiss motorway vignette?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Yes. Any car using Swiss motorways needs a vignette (CHF 40, valid for the calendar year). You can buy it online at <a href="https://www.e-vignette.ch/en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-blue-600 underline">e-vignette.ch</a>, at border petrol stations, or at Swiss post offices. Fines for not having one are steep (CHF 200+).</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Is Mount Rigi or Mount Pilatus better with young kids?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600"><strong>Rigi is easier.</strong> The summit is lower (1,798m vs 2,132m), warmer, and has a playground. Paths are gentler. Pilatus is more dramatic but colder, windier, and the cogwheel carriages require folding strollers. If you can only do one, Rigi is the safer bet with toddlers. If you can do both, Rigi first (easier) then Pilatus (the wow factor).</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Weggis or Vitznau — which village should I stay in?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Both are great. <strong>Vitznau</strong> is quieter, has the historic cogwheel railway to Rigi, and is slightly better for families with young kids who need nap-friendly calm. <strong>Weggis</strong> has more restaurants, a longer promenade, and slightly more evening atmosphere. They're a 10-minute boat ride apart, so you'll visit both regardless.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">How much does the Pilatus golden round trip cost?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Adults ~CHF 111 for the full golden round trip (boat + cogwheel + gondola). Children under 6 are free. Ages 6–15 get ~50% off. There are family tickets available. Check <a href="https://www.pilatus.ch/en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-blue-600 underline">pilatus.ch</a> for current prices — they change annually.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Is the Pilatus cogwheel railway open in late May?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Usually yes. It typically opens mid-to-late April each year, weather permitting. By late May it should be fully operational. Always confirm on the <a href="https://www.pilatus.ch/en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-blue-600 underline">Pilatus website</a> before your trip. The Kriens gondola runs year-round as a backup route.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Why stock up in Germany before crossing into Switzerland?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Swiss prices for everyday items (nappies, baby food, medicine, snacks) are 40–60% higher than Germany. DM and Rossmann are the best drugstores — stock up on Day 3 in the Black Forest area. You'll re-enter Germany on Day 8, so you can top up again then.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Can I take a stroller on the mountain trains and boats?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Yes, with caveats. Lake boats have step-free boarding — strollers roll right on. The Rigi cogwheel and cable car accommodate unfolded strollers. Pilatus gondolas take strollers, but the cogwheel carriages are tighter — fold the stroller before boarding. All summit terraces are flat and stroller-friendly.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">What currency does Switzerland use?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Swiss Francs (CHF). Cards are widely accepted in towns, restaurants, and transport. Smaller villages and parking meters sometimes need cash. ATMs are available at train stations and in Lucerne. Some places near the border accept EUR but give change in CHF at a poor rate — better to use CHF.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Should I drive into Lucerne city?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">No — parking is expensive and the old town is easier on foot. Take the lake steamer from Weggis or Vitznau instead. It's stroller-accessible, drops you in the city centre, and avoids parking stress entirely. A day pass may save money if you're doing multiple boat trips.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Are shops open on Saturday evening and Sunday in Germany?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Mostly no. German shops close early on Saturday afternoon and are closed on Sunday. If your return route passes through a German town on Saturday (like Königswinter), stock up en route or before shops shut. Petrol station shops are the exception — they're open but pricier.</p>
    </div>
  </details>
</div>
