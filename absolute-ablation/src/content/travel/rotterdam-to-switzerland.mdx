We're taking our family — three adults, a five-year-old, and a one-year-old — on a road trip from Rotterdam to Switzerland in May 2026. Eight days, four stops, one loop through the Black Forest, Rhine Falls, and Lake Lucerne. Waterfalls first, mountains second — that's the priority. I've spent weeks planning this route to keep every driving leg under five hours, avoid steep hikes, and build in enough rest days that nobody melts down (adults included). This post is the full plan — day-by-day itinerary, costs, packing lists, and everything I wish someone had written down before I started researching.

**Priority hierarchy:** Waterfalls > Mountains (cable car/cog railway) > Caves > Lakes (boats, promenades) > Cities

<SectionHeading icon="fa-route" color="blue">The Route</SectionHeading>

<div class="prose-content">

Our route heads south from Rotterdam through Germany — straight to Triberg for Germany's highest waterfall, then Rhine Falls (Europe's largest by volume) on the way into Switzerland for five nights at Lake Lucerne, then back north via Königswinter on the Rhine. The first leg is the longest at five hours; after that, every drive stays under four.

<Callout type="insight" title="Weather-based day swapping" icon="fa-cloud-sun">
  <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Days 4–6 (Rigi, Lauterbrunnen, Pilatus) can be reordered to chase the best weather. Day 2 (Triberg + Rhine Falls) is fixed — it's on the transit day. Clear days go to Rigi and Pilatus (need views). Grey or rainy days go to Lauterbrunnen — Trümmelbach Falls is underground and Staubbach is dramatic in rain. Triberg Waterfalls work in any weather — more spectacular in rain.</p>
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Supply strategy" icon="fa-shopping-cart">
  <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Day 1 (Saturday) is a long drive to Triberg — <strong>stock up at a motorway DM/Rossmann en route or in Triberg Saturday evening before shops close (~8 PM).</strong> Day 2 (Sunday) has shops closed. Critical: buy nappies, baby food, and medicine at DM/Rossmann in Germany — Swiss prices are 40–60% higher. Re-stock in Germany on Day 7 (Königswinter/Bonn area).</p>

  <StepItem number={5} title="★ Lauterbrunnen Valley Day Trip — Wed 28 May">
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-600 leading-relaxed"><strong>~1h15 drive each way</strong> via the Brünig Pass (toll-free, scenic). The Lauterbrunnen Valley is one of the deepest glacial valleys in the Alps — 72 waterfalls pour off vertical cliffs. Two headline stops:</p>

Allow 1–1.5h.</p>
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-600 leading-relaxed mt-2"><strong>Staubbach Falls</strong> — 297m free-falling waterfall visible from the village. Completely free. Flat walk from parking to the viewpoint. One of the most photographed waterfalls in Switzerland.</p>

<SectionHeading icon="fa-mountain" color="emerald">Lake Lucerne: The Main Base</SectionHeading>

<div class="prose-content">

Five nights at Lake Lucerne is the heart of the trip. Pick a lakeside village as a base for everything — the Rigi day trip, the Lauterbrunnen Valley waterfalls, the Pilatus golden round trip, and a rest day. Both Weggis and Vitznau have flat waterfront paths — good for strollers and anyone who prefers level ground.

</div>

<SectionHeading icon="fa-circle-question" color="purple">FAQ</SectionHeading>

<div class="space-y-4 my-6">
  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Do I need a Swiss motorway vignette?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Yes. Any car using Swiss motorways needs a vignette (CHF 40, valid for the calendar year). You can buy it online at <a href="https://www.e-vignette.ch/en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-blue-600 underline">e-vignette.ch</a>, at border petrol stations, or at Swiss post offices. Fines for not having one are steep (CHF 200+).</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Is Mount Rigi or Mount Pilatus better with young kids?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600"><strong>Rigi is easier.</strong> The summit is lower (1,798m vs 2,132m), warmer, and has a playground. Paths are gentler. Pilatus is more dramatic but colder, windier, and the cogwheel carriages require folding strollers. If you can only do one, Rigi is the safer bet with toddlers. If you can do both, Rigi first (easier) then Pilatus (the wow factor).</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Weggis or Vitznau — which village should I stay in?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Both are great. <strong>Vitznau</strong> is quieter, has the historic cogwheel railway to Rigi, and is slightly better for families with young kids who need nap-friendly calm. <strong>Weggis</strong> has more restaurants, a longer promenade, and slightly more evening atmosphere. They're a 10-minute boat ride apart, so you'll visit both regardless.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">How much does the Pilatus golden round trip cost?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Adults ~CHF 111 for the full golden round trip (boat + cogwheel + gondola). Children under 6 are free. Ages 6–15 get ~50% off. There are family tickets available. Check <a href="https://www.pilatus.ch/en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-blue-600 underline">pilatus.ch</a> for current prices — they change annually.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Is the Pilatus cogwheel railway open in late May?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Usually yes. It typically opens mid-to-late April each year, weather permitting. By late May it should be fully operational. Always confirm on the <a href="https://www.pilatus.ch/en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-blue-600 underline">Pilatus website</a> before your trip. The Kriens gondola runs year-round as a backup route.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Why stock up in Germany before crossing into Switzerland?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Swiss prices for everyday items (nappies, baby food, medicine, snacks) are 40–60% higher than Germany. DM and Rossmann are the best drugstores — stock up at a motorway service station on Day 1 or in Triberg Saturday evening. You'll re-enter Germany on Day 7, so you can top up again then.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Can I take a stroller on the mountain trains and boats?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Yes, with caveats. Lake boats have step-free boarding — strollers roll right on. The Rigi cogwheel and cable car accommodate unfolded strollers. Pilatus gondolas take strollers, but the cogwheel carriages are tighter — fold the stroller before boarding. All summit terraces are flat and stroller-friendly. <strong>Exception:</strong> Triberg Waterfalls and Trümmelbach Falls require a baby carrier — trails are natural/narrow/wet. Rhine Falls north side and Staubbach Falls are stroller-accessible.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Is Trümmelbach Falls senior-friendly?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Mostly yes. A tunnel lift takes you up inside the mountain — no climbing required. The interior walkways are uneven and wet, so wear grippy shoes and go slowly. The spray is heavy in places. It's not strenuous, but it's not a flat promenade either. Seniors with reasonable mobility will manage fine. If in doubt, the lower falls (accessible from the lift) are the most impressive — you can skip the upper levels.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">What currency does Switzerland use?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Swiss Francs (CHF). Cards are widely accepted in towns, restaurants, and transport. Smaller villages and parking meters sometimes need cash. ATMs are available at train stations and in Lucerne. Some places near the border accept EUR but give change in CHF at a poor rate — better to use CHF.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Should I drive into Lucerne city?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">No — parking is expensive and the old town is easier on foot. Take the lake steamer from Weggis or Vitznau instead. It's stroller-accessible, drops you in the city centre, and avoids parking stress entirely. A day pass may save money if you're doing multiple boat trips.</p>
    </div>
  </details>

  <details class="bg-white border border-gray-200 rounded-lg">
    <summary class="cursor-pointer px-4 py-3 text-sm font-semibold text-gray-800 hover:bg-gray-50">Are shops open on Saturday evening and Sunday in Germany?</summary>
    <div class="px-4 pb-3">
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-600">Mostly no. German shops close early on Saturday afternoon and are closed on Sunday. Your return passes through Königswinter on Friday (Day 7) — shops will be open, so stock up then. Petrol station shops are the exception — they're open on weekends but pricier.</p>
    </div>
  </details>
</div>
